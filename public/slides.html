During Session 1

Why nation-building gets loud

A country houses only about half its workforce to begin with, and an unhoused worker cannot be employed. Homes cost materials and food — so the Build step is where groups discover they cannot do this alone.

🏘️

Build before Industry

The step order is the lesson. A group that skips housing arrives at Industry unable to staff what it planned, and the app tells it exactly how many people have nowhere to live.

🤝

They can trade right away

There is a trade panel on the Build step — they do not have to wait for the mass game. Expect groups to leave their seats and go looking for materials.

⚖️

Three homelands cannot finish alone

River Delta is one material short, Port City is short of both, Dry Plains is short of food. That is deliberate — it is what gives trade something to be about.

If a group says they are stuck, they probably are — and that is correct.

The app says so on screen: "Your homeland does not have enough to house everyone — that is not a mistake." Point them at another group rather than at a fix. A country that launches with people unhoused is not broken; it will build its way out once industries start paying.

This lever only exists on a classroom console — a hall console does not have it.

Afterwards

The debrief is the lesson

Once the game master has run Session 2 and shown the final table, the scoreboard is the hook back in your own classroom. These three questions are what they take home.

💸

Cheap then, expensive later?

Which decision felt like a bargain at the time and cost you dearly by round five?

🤝

Who did you trade with?

And why them? What did you have that they needed — and what did that cost you?

🔁

What would you change?

If you started again tomorrow with the same homeland, what would you do differently?

Harmony is not decoration.

It multiplies the income every industry earns. A divided country literally collects less money from the same factories — worth pointing at on the final table.

During the hall, each round

The rhythm of a round

The same four beats, up to five times over. Your job is the pacing — the game does the rest.

🎴

Throw the card

One scenario hits every country in the room at once. Read it aloud; the console shows it on their screens too.

🗣️

The first minute is yours

For sixty seconds after a card lands, nobody can decide, trade or make an alliance — the server refuses all three and every phone counts it down. Read the story into that minute. It is the only part of the round where the room has nothing else to do.

⏱️

Let them argue — on the clock

Five ministers, one decision. Set Decision time to 1, 2 or 3 minutes and every card starts the clock by itself; students see the same countdown on their own screens. Do not rescue them — the disagreement is where the thinking happens.

🤝

Open the floor to trade

No country can build everything alone. Groups can already trade from the Build step, but this is when alliances start to matter — they make co-operative scenario choices far stronger. Defence Ministers propose alliances and Trade Ministers send goods. Each picks the other country by name on the Trade / Alliance tab, and the other side has to accept.

▶️

Press Next round

The clock running out does not advance anything — it turns red and tells you how many countries are still deciding. You press the button when you are ready. Then income is collected, meters move and the leaderboard reshuffles.

Resist the urge to warn them.

Cheap policy cards are cheap for a reason, and nothing is labelled "bad". Groups only discover the cost when the meters move — and that discovery is the whole point. A country that never decides at all loses Stability and Harmony: refusing to govern is its own choice.

Setting one up from scratch

Deploy to Railway

1

Push this folder to a new GitHub repository

Railway builds straight from it.

2

Railway: New Project → Deploy from GitHub repo

If the repo does not appear, the Railway GitHub App has not been given access — authorise it from the repo picker.

3

Nothing to configure

Railway detects Node via NIXPACKS and runs npm start. No environment variables are required — the server reads process.env.PORT on its own.

4

Settings → Networking → Generate Domain

That URL is what the class uses for /play and you use for /host.

5

Settings → Volumes → add a volume mounted at /data

The server finds it on its own. Without it, rooms survive a restart but not a redeploy.

GET /health returns ok — point Railway's health check at that path.

The master switch

ADMIN_KEY and the /admin panel

Set ADMIN_KEY in Railway's variables — 16 characters or more. Unset, or shorter than that, and /admin loads but every action on it refuses.

/admin
📋

List rooms

Code, kind, label, how many countries and how many committed, phase and round, and when it was last touched. No host keys and no country codes — this is for triage, not for reading over somebody's shoulder.

💾

Download everything

Every room on the server, exported as one JSON file — the same shape /host can restore a single room from.

🗑

Delete selected

Tick rooms, confirm, gone — flushed to disk immediately, not on the usual delay.

☢️

Delete everything

Type DELETE to arm the button. Deletes every room on the server, including any game in progress.

Belt and braces

Backups & restore

Every change is written to rooms.json about a second later, and read back when the server starts — a room survives a restart on its own.

💾

From the console

💾 Save backup on /host downloads the one room a teacher or game master is running.

🗝️

From /admin

Download everything backs up every room on the server in one file.

📂

Restore

📂 Restore from file on /host's setup screen puts a room back with the same room code and the same country codes. While the room is still open this needs nothing extra; a room that has actually closed (volume lost, an /admin wipe, or a teacher's own Reset) needs the admin key too.

⚠️ The volume is what makes any of this survive a redeploy.

Mount it at /data. Without it, a restart still keeps rooms — a redeploy does not.

The one rule that matters most

One replica, always

Rooms live in this process's memory. If Railway runs two copies of the server, half the class lands on a copy that has never heard of the room code.

Settings → Deploy → Replicas: 1.

Do not switch on horizontal autoscaling. One replica carries a whole cohort comfortably.

📶

One student poll

1.2 KB gzipped — or a 304 with no body at all when nothing has changed.

🏟️

250 devices at once

Served in 253 ms, 306 KB total, measured with 50 groups in one room.

🧠

50 groups in memory

62 MB. There is plenty of headroom on one replica.

Tuning it

/data if it exists, else ./data. Where rooms.json is written.

🗓️

Default 14. How long an untouched room is kept before it is swept.

🗝️

Unset by default. 16 or more characters turns /admin on.

Step six, in detail

House your people before you hire them

Your country starts with only about half its workers housed. The rest have nowhere to live — and a worker with nowhere to live cannot work.

🏠

Homes cost materials and food

Not points. −2 ⛏️ −2 🌾 buys a home that houses two more workers. Homes are always available, whatever your ministers chose.

🗂️

Your policies unlock the rest

Housing estates, MRT stations, solar farms, coal plants, parks and places of worship each need the Infrastructure card that names them. No card, no building.

🤝

Some homelands cannot finish alone

If you run out of materials, that is not a mistake. Use the trade panel on the Build step and go find a country with materials to spare.

You do not have to wait for the mass game to trade.

River Delta has food coming out of its ears and almost no materials. The Highlands has the opposite problem. Find each other early — the countries that trade first get to build first.

Watch out

Cheap cards are cheap for a reason

Nothing in this game is labelled "bad". The cheapest policy always buys you something — and quietly costs you something else. You will only find out when the meters move.

🌍

You cannot build everything

Every homeland starts short of something. Countries that trade early do better than countries that hoard.

🤝

Harmony pays actual money

It multiplies what every industry earns. A divided country collects less from the same factories.

💰 20 coins to start 💠 24 points to split 🎴 5 rounds ⚠️ Every choice has a cost
The mass game

Five rounds. Everything goes wrong.

Haze, riots, brain drain, raiders, drought, a foreign firm with a tempting offer. Every country in the room gets the same card at the same time.

🗣️

Argue it out

Discuss as a cabinet. The Leader has the final say — one choice only.

🤝

Trade & ally

Defence proposes alliances. Trade sends goods. Pick the other country from the list on the Trade / Alliance tab — they have to accept. Alliances make co-operative choices far stronger.

📊

Watch the meters

Your city on screen changes with them — buildings, traffic, crowds, weather, smog.

⏱️ You may be on a clock — and doing nothing is not a safe option.

Your teacher can put 1, 2 or 3 minutes on each card. The countdown shows at the top of your screen. A country that runs out of time without agreeing on anything loses Stability and Harmony — sitting on your hands costs you more than making an imperfect choice. Decide, even if you are not sure.

How you actually win

Your weakest pillar drags everything down

Six pillars are scored. Your overall score is pulled toward the lowest one — so a country that is brilliant at four things and hopeless at two will lose to a steady one.
